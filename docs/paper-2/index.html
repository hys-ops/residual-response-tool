An exact recurrence for the optimal query depth of FBDD templates

Two free binary decision diagrams share a set of unknown configuration bits. When their equivalence predicate factors through binary parity signals, its signal truth table and the multiplicity of each column determine the minimum number of physical setting bits that any exact deterministic strategy must read in the worst case.

From signals to the optimal strategy

Write the predicate as E(θ) = g(Mθ) over the binary field. A column records which signals a single physical bit changes. Let m(W) count all copies of column types lying in W. The recurrence starts on the span of all columns. At a reachable affine signal space z + W, its value is zero if g is constant. Otherwise it minimizes, over supported hyperplanes H of W, the sum of:

  1. m(W) − m(H), the number of bits read to cut down to H;
  2. the larger optimum of the two resulting affine cosets of H.

A supported hyperplane is spanned by the present columns it contains. The minimum gives both the exact depth and an optimal strategy, using the physical bit locations to perform its reads. After a representation is supplied, evaluation takes 2O(r²) poly(k,r) time for signal rank r and k configuration bits. A valid representation always exists, but it need not have small rank or be cheap to extract. Paper 1 measures the time to decide equivalence for two already filled diagrams; this paper primarily counts probes to unknown settings, not that runtime.

Proof map and focused notes

1. Normalize arbitrary physical strategies

As long as the unread columns still span the same space, the answer of a read only translates its signal offset within that space. At the first span drop, bits outside the resulting hyperplane can be read first without raising worst-case depth.

Focused proof (PDF)

2. Resolve overlapping parities

Each nonzero column in the three-signal space appears m times. The first cut costs 4m; nonaffinity forces a second cut costing another 2m on some branch. Reading the selector signal first attains the bound.

Focused proof (PDF)

3. Execute a strategy without solving MaxCut

For a connected graph template, read the nonroot colors and then crossing-edge data. The exact worst-case depth is |V| − 1 + MaxCut(G). Running the strategy does not require finding a maximum cut; predicting the depth is NP-hard.

Focused proof (PDF)

4. Predict depth from explicit wiring

With exactly three settings, predicting optimal depth and deciding equivalence of a pair of filled FBDDs are polynomial-time Turing equivalent. The proof distinguishes depths 2 versus 3; it is not a circuit-size or unrestricted equivalence-time lower bound.

Focused proof (PDF)

What Lean verifies

The archived Lean 4.19.0 replay records a clean build of 18 modules and an audit of 18 declarations. TemplateDepth.optimal_depth_eq_phi and phi_bellman connect ordinary physical-bit optimal depth to the recurrence. Selector.physical_selector_depth and Graph.graph_optimal verify the selector and graph-predicate depths. Prediction.three_bit_depth and Prediction.selector_semantics check the numerical depths and Boolean predicate of the three-setting construction.

The raw wiring extraction, finite-enumeration runtime, graph wiring realization and NP-completeness corollary, and polynomial-size/eight-call details of the wiring reduction are written arguments, not verified Lean computational claims. Python checks are supplied separately. The project reuses the diagram semantics module from Paper 1; the optimization theorem is distinct.
