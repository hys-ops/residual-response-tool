A common quadratic-size test for all block restrictions

Technical ingredient in the improved upper bound

The question

Fix the outside of a variable block. Every outside assignment leaves a possibly different polynomial. Can one short list detect all the nonzero possibilities?

How it works

  1. A topologically ordered FBDD evaluation is linear in earlier node values except for a multiplication by the query name at each in-block occurrence. Compress other computations into field coefficients: k such occurrences yield a triangular recurrence with O(k²) coefficient slots.
  2. Enumerate the short descriptions. A nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree at most k is zero at at most a k/p fraction of field points; greedily choose points that hit the largest share of the remaining descriptions. If M descriptions are enumerated, M ≤ pO(k²). After s greedy choices at most M(k/p)s remain. Taking p > k² ensures log(p/k) ≥ ½ log p (and k=1 is immediate), so O(k²) choices suffice.
  3. To see the order is sharp, one fixed FBDD permits arbitrary bilinear coefficient matrices after outside restriction. A test point contributes one linear constraint on those coefficients. Too few points leave a nonzero coefficient choice invisible.

What carries forward

Separate heavily repeated names and use the product of common lists over blocks of light names. This is a direct ingredient in the 2O(N2/3 log(N+2)) bit-time result. Lean checks the common test, local sharpness, correctness and Boolean witness; bit scheduling remains written.
